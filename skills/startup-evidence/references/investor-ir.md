# Investor IR Contract

Create an investor memo, editable deck, or data-room outline only from reconciled evidence. Route document generation through the document skill and PPTX/Slides generation through the presentation skill; both require render-based visual QA.

## Freeze the audience and ask

Record company stage, investor type, geography, round or purpose, requested amount, intended use, meeting context, artifact format, and `as of` date. If the ask or stage is unknown, flag it before optimizing the narrative.

## Maintain numerical truth

- Label every number as `actual`, `estimate`, `forecast`, or `target`.
- Give actuals a measurement period, definition, and evidence source.
- Give estimates and forecasts formulas, assumptions, and scenario labels.
- Reconcile every repeated figure across narrative, charts, tables, speaker notes, and appendices.
- Show currency, units, cohort/period, gross-versus-net treatment, and rounding.
- Never fabricate traction, customers, logos, partnerships, pipeline, retention, market size, valuation, or investor interest.
- Avoid false precision. Missing data remains an explicit gap.

For market sizing, prefer a transparent bottom-up model tied to the target segment and purchasing unit. If a top-down number is useful context, keep it separate. Do not turn a broad industry total into the serviceable market without a defensible bridge.

## Recommended narrative sequence

Adapt the sequence to the company rather than forcing every section into a slide:

1. company and concise thesis;
2. problem and affected customer;
3. current alternatives and why they fall short;
4. solution and demonstrated workflow;
5. positioning and differentiated value;
6. market and bottom-up sizing;
7. evidence or traction;
8. business model and unit economics;
9. go-to-market and sales motion;
10. competition and durable advantage;
11. team and relevant ability to execute;
12. ask, use of funds, milestones, and material risks.

Distinguish observed proof from planned milestones. Include counterevidence and risk where it changes the investment decision. A confident story can still be honest about uncertainty.

## Deterministic checks

At minimum, verify:

- totals, percentages, growth rates, runway, and use-of-funds arithmetic;
- time periods and cohort definitions;
- market-sizing formulas and unit conversions;
- consistency of repeated metrics and the ask;
- chart values against source tables;
- source notes for external claims;
- editable artifact parse, font/overflow, clipping, contrast, and slide/page count.

Do not claim that visual polish or an AI verifier establishes investment readiness. High-stakes legal, tax, securities, and financial statements require qualified human review.

## Minimum handoff

Return the artifact, source/claim index, metrics reconciliation table, assumptions and gaps, independent verification report, rendered visual-QA result, and skipped checks. If the underlying data is not sufficient, return a diligence-ready outline and data request rather than invented slides.

# Competitive Intelligence Contract

Use one comparison scope and one claim ledger for the deep dive, matrix, and battle cards.

## Choose the comparison set

Start from the customer's real alternatives rather than a list of companies that look similar. Include only relevant members of these classes and label each class:

- direct competitor;
- adjacent product;
- incumbent or bundled suite;
- internal build or services;
- manual workflow;
- doing nothing.

Record an inclusion reason, excluded near-matches, geography, customer segment, and `as of` date. Do not rank alternatives before defining the buyer, user, job, and buying situation.

## Build each deep dive

For every included alternative, cover:

1. product and target job;
2. ideal customer, buyer, user, and trigger;
3. workflow and evidenced capabilities;
4. public pricing and commercial constraints;
5. go-to-market signals and customer-authored proof;
6. genuine strengths, limitations, switching costs, and unknowns;
7. implications for the focal company, separated into inference and recommendation.

Treat vendor copy as evidence of what the vendor claims, not proof of customer value. Treat reviews and social posts as anecdotes unless the sampling method supports a broader claim. Never infer revenue, funding, adoption, or satisfaction from web presence alone.

## Normalize price and feature comparisons

Capture the commercial unit before comparing numbers:

- plan and product version;
- monthly or annual billing;
- currency, tax inclusion, and observed date;
- per-seat, per-workspace, per-unit, usage, or hybrid basis;
- minimum seats or contract value;
- included allowance, overage, discount, trial, and cancellation limits;
- region and customer segment.

Recommended matrix columns:

| Alternative | Plan/version | Comparable unit | Public price | Included usage | Overage/minimum | Feature evidence | Source date | Confidence/gap |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Use `contact sales` or `not publicly available` when appropriate. Do not estimate a private price merely to complete the table. Separate `documented`, `observed hands-on`, `beta`, `announced roadmap`, `third-party report`, and `inferred` feature states. A feature with no current primary evidence is not a confirmed feature.

If units are not directly comparable, show the normalization formula and assumptions. Keep original prices beside normalized prices and mark the result as an estimate.

## Produce useful battle cards

Create one card per buying alternative, not one generic attack sheet. Include:

- when and in which segment it appears;
- why a reasonable customer might choose it;
- genuine strengths and credible weaknesses;
- situations in which the focal product should win or should lose;
- neutral discovery questions;
- likely objections, concise responses, and the proof required for each response;
- switching and implementation considerations;
- a watchlist of claims likely to change.

Link each decision-relevant line to a claim ID or label it as a hypothesis to validate. Avoid fear, uncertainty, and doubt, unprovable superlatives, or attacks on people. A battle card is decision support, not permission to misrepresent a competitor.

## Minimum handoff

Return the selection logic, deep dives, normalized matrix, requested battle cards, the shared claim ledger, and explicit evidence gaps. When live research was unavailable, state that prices and current features remain unverified rather than presenting remembered values.
